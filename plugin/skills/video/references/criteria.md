# Editorial criteria and comparison cases

These are original editorial examples, not borrowed coach quotes or licensed video sources.

Good A: A career coach demonstrates "situation, action, result" using one specific interview answer. The hook says "Replace a vague interview answer with one example." The clip includes the example and warns against inventing metrics. The viewer can revise one answer immediately.

Good B: A manager coach shows how to describe an observable missed deadline and ask what blocked it. The hook says "Start feedback with what happened, not a label." Preserve the qualification that urgent safety concerns require escalation. The next step is a two-line feedback template.

Bad: "This sentence guarantees a promotion in 7 days." The source describes one person's experience, not a guarantee. Reject the hook even if it is shorter or more dramatic. Cropping out the demonstration or removing its qualification also fails.

Score source fidelity, fulfilled promise, useful action, coach voice, readable presentation and achievable CTA from 0 (fails) to 2 (clear). Any fabricated claim, lost qualification or unauthorized use fails regardless of total score. Record evidence, not just a number. Hook evaluation also needs an audience member to explain expected benefit and why they would continue; no invented retention estimates.

Compare the plugin against ordinary Claude on the same source and model: clear lesson, repetitive long discussion with a qualification, then a held-out second coach/topic. Fix criteria before the held-out case. Blind-label outputs, record model/instruction/runtime versions, judge each dimension and coach editing time. Prefer the simpler instruction when quality ties. Mechanical tests and this rubric do not establish comparative superiority. No comparative run or human acceptance is claimed in this package.
